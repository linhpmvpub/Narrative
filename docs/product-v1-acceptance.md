# Product v1: scope and measurable acceptance

Implementation sequencing update2026-11-25: [core profile v1](core-profile-v1.md) fixes7groups/10relations for initial expansion. Complete the bounded database and algorithm before comprehensive evaluation; do not require a separate LLM-prior evaluation now. Release-quality targets below remain future acceptance targets, not a gate preventing current authorized implementation or a claim of measured readiness.

The first product covers MU, NVDA, AMD, DELL and SNDK. It surfaces source-linked developments that plausibly matter to selected company narratives, including supply chains, input costs, demand, finance, policy and infrastructure. It does not predict stock prices or equate relevance with verified impact. SNDK and WDC remain distinct identities. The canonical seed's ten templates are unchanged; private company dossiers can have additional explicit scopes.

`config/product-v1.json` is the versioned working acceptance policy. These are engineering targets set before the next evaluation, not achieved metrics. Changing thresholds requires a new policy version and a new freeze; do not lower a gate after seeing labels.

1. Scope: each narrative declares included developments, exclusions, source requirements and unknowns. Product shipments are separate from foundry capacity; general demand is separate from a named customer/order. Broad theme discovery is not company exposure.
2. Profiles: all five issuers need hash-verified, time-versioned dossiers. Ten required component areas must be represented explicitly as sourced or unknown; identity and products must be sourced. At least three driver areas beyond identity/products must have evidence. Missing DELL/SNDK coverage blocks the full five-company beta. An evidence checklist is not independent approval.
3. Events: support bounded candidate detection for ten event families, including correction/withdrawal and insider activity. Candidate detection never creates canonical facts. Actual assertion, speaker, numeric period, named counterpart and lifecycle checks remain separate downstream gates. Unsupported syntax stays unknown/review-required.
4. Collection: durable incremental RSS/Atom and SEC discovery, raw-capture hashes, revisions, conditional requests, retry/backoff, bounded concurrency and recovery. Monitor publisher/source coverage, stale sources, undated items and queue backlog. Polling is near-real-time, not a guaranteed live wire. Source reach does not prove recall.
5. Quality: at least100 distinct event clusters,40resolved positive pairs,80resolved negatives,10resolved relevant clusters per target company and5topic strata. Precision>=0.85, precision Wilson95% lower bound>=0.75, recall>=0.70, unresolved<=20%. Report counts and per-company failures; article/narrative pairs from one event are correlated. Wilson intervals are descriptive pair-level bounds, not cluster-adjusted certainty. Genuine product release also requires a seven-day operational observation (>=95% successful attempts, <=10% stale sources) and human validation for public launch. Two independent AI reviewers remain acceptable for development/private research, clearly labeled.

All review labels must be collected after a prediction freeze with separate reviewer contexts. Exclude prior economic periods, near-duplicate articles and training/development exposures. Current-profile archival tests and prospective live-news tests are separate cohorts and must not be pooled into one release score. Scope changes create a new target; old target labels cannot be reassigned. Preserve failed and unresolved cases.

Implementation and acceptance are separate. A pipeline may be implemented and tested while the five-company profile or quality gate remains BLOCKED. Real notifications and cloud data remain disabled; no gate can enable them. Public launch, hosting and notification activation are outside steps1–5.
